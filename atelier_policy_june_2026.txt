VIVO FASHION GROUP
In-Store Garment Alterations Service

1.  PURPOSE

This policy outlines the standards, scope, pricing, and procedures governing in-store garment alteration services at selected Vivo retail outlets. The Junction store is the initial pilot location for this service.

Objectives
Enhance customer experience through improved garment fit
Support sales conversion and increase basket value
Ensure operational control, quality, and consistency

2.  SERVICE OVERVIEW

Vivo offers professional in-store garment alteration services for customers who have purchased Vivo garments. This service is:

A paid service, with defined exceptions (see Section 3.3)
Delivered within a controlled and defined scope
Designed to support retail sales — not to operate as a full tailoring service

4.  SCOPE OF SERVICE

4.1  Included Services
Hemming (pants, skirts, dresses)
Sleeve shortening
Minor waist adjustments
Basic size adjustments (within one size limit)
Light tapering for fit improvement
Minor repairs: button reattachment (done manually in-store), simple seam repairs, and basic zip fixes

4.2  Excluded Services
Major resizing beyond one size
Full garment reconstruction or redesign
Structural alterations (e.g. shoulders, full lining adjustments)
Complex fabric work (e.g. heavy embellishments, delicate materials beyond capability)
Repair of heavily damaged garments
Alterations on non-Vivo garments — see eligibility note below

Where a replacement component (zip, button, thread colour) is not available in-store, the tailor must raise a materials request to the production unit before confirming the job with the customer. The turnaround extends to 48 hours in these cases. Requests must follow the formal requisition process — verbal requests to the factory are not acceptable.

Garments purchased on the day at Junction: eligible for both paid and free-threshold service.
Vivo garments purchased previously: eligible for paid alterations only — customer must present proof of purchase (receipt or order record).
Garments with no verifiable Vivo origin: must be declined. Do not accept on the customer's verbal assurance alone.

5.  SERVICE LEVELS (SLAS)

5.1  Standard Turnaround
All alterations to be completed within 24 hours (next-day collection, excluding weekends), where all required thread and materials are in stock at the store. Where materials must be sourced from the production unit, the turnaround extends to 48 hours. The tailor must confirm material availability at the point of intake and communicate the applicable timeline to the customer before payment is taken.

5.2  Same-Day Hemming
Limited to simple hemming only
Cut-off time: 2:00 PM
Subject to capacity and store traffic

5.3  Peak Period Adjustments
During weekends, promotions, and high-traffic periods: same-day service may be suspended
Turnaround may extend to 48 hours during peak periods

5.4  Capacity Management
Maximum 8–12 garments per day per tailor
Maximum 2 alterations per garment
Once capacity is reached, new requests must be deferred to the next available slot — do not over-commit

6.  STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE (SOP)

All alteration jobs must follow these steps in sequence. No step may be skipped.

STEP 1 / RequestAssessment | ▸  Confirm request falls within approved scope (Section 4.1) / ▸  Escalate unclear or out-of-scope requests to Store Manager
STEP 2 / Measurement& Marking | ▸  Take accurate measurements / ▸  Clearly mark the garment / ▸  Confirm the expected outcome with the customer verbally
STEP 3 / Job Logging& Tagging | ▸  Record job in the Alterations Log: customer details, garment description, alteration required, promised collection date / ▸  Attach a matching physical tag to the garment — no untagged garments in the queue
STEP 4 / CustomerAgreement& Payment | ▸  Complete the Customer Agreement Form — document the alteration and agreed expected outcome / ▸  Confirm collection timeline with the customer / ▸  Collect payment before work begins — process through POS / ▸  MANDATORY: The Customer Agreement Form must be signed/acknowledged before any work commences. Verbal-only agreements are not accepted.
STEP 5 / Execution | ▸  Complete alterations to defined quality standards / ▸  Adhere strictly to the documented instructions — do not deviate without customer consent
STEP 6 / QualityCheck | ▸  Inspect for accuracy against the Customer Agreement Form / ▸  Check for clean finish with neatly trimmed threads / ▸  Press all altered areas before storage / ▸  Confirm no garment damage
STEP 7 / Collection& Handover | ▸  Customer to try on the garment where possible / ▸  Confirm satisfaction before releasing the garment / ▸  Package in a Vivo-branded bag — this is the standard for all completed alterations

Where the customer did not clearly specify the expected outcome at intake and no written Customer Agreement Form was completed, Vivo is not obligated to provide free rework. This is why completion of the Customer Agreement Form (Step 4 of the SOP) is a mandatory pre-condition for all alteration work.

7.  CUSTOMER AGREEMENT & SERVICE CONDITIONS

All alterations must be agreed and documented before work begins
Changes after commencement may incur additional charges
Perfect fit is not guaranteed where garment construction limitations exist
Vivo reserves the right to decline requests outside the defined scope

8.  REWORK, REFUND & LIABILITY

8.1  Rework
Incorrect alterations will be corrected at no additional cost, where Vivo is at fault. "Incorrect" is defined as: the alteration was not executed in accordance with what was documented on the Customer Agreement Form signed at intake.

8.2  Customer-Initiated Changes
Any changes requested after completion will be treated as a new service and charged accordingly.

8.3  Refunds
No refunds once work is completed, except in verified quality failures attributable to Vivo.

8.4  Liability
Vivo will take reasonable care in handling all customer garments
Any damage must be reported to the Store Manager immediately upon discovery
The Store Manager will assess and escalate as required
Vivo's maximum liability for garment damage caused during an alteration is capped at the original retail purchase price of the affected garment, evidenced by receipt
Resolution options: repair, replacement, or compensation up to the liability cap
Compensation above the cap requires escalation to and approval from the Head of Retail
Cash refunds of the alteration fee are considered only where Vivo is demonstrably at fault and the garment cannot be repaired or replaced to the customer's reasonable satisfaction

9.  ROLES & RESPONSIBILITIES

TAILOR
• Execute alterations accurately, on time, and to defined quality standards
• Complete the Customer Agreement Form and job log for every job — no exceptions
• Confirm material availability at intake and escalate shortages to the Store Manager immediately
• Complete the weekly handover log before the end of each rotation week
• Press and package all completed garments in Vivo-branded bags before storage
• Manage the work queue and flag capacity constraints proactively

STORE MANAGER
• Oversee service delivery, quality, and workflow daily
• Enforce pricing, scope, SLAs, and the Customer Agreement Form requirement
• Approve exceptions where necessary and handle escalations and disputes
• Review the alterations log daily and follow up on uncollected garments within 48 hours
• Submit weekly thread and materials replenishment requests to the production unit by Wednesday
• Manage the weekly tailor rotation, including absence cover
• Track paid versus free-service revenue weekly and report to Retail leadership

SALES TEAM
• Clearly communicate the service scope and pricing — do not over-promise
• Direct customers to the tailor for all scope and timeline questions
• Do not initiate alteration agreements verbally — all jobs must be formally logged
• Apply the garment eligibility criteria accurately and consistently

PRODUCTION UNIT
• Supply materials requests submitted by Wednesday for delivery by Friday each week
• Ensure seconded tailors are available as per the rotation schedule
• Provide an absence replacement by 8:00 AM where a scheduled tailor cannot attend
• Monitor the impact of secondments on Sample Making output and escalate if capacity is affected

11.  RISK & CONTROL MEASURES

CONTROL | DETAIL
Garment tagging & tracking | All garments must be tagged at intake and tracked through to collection. No untagged garment may enter the work queue.
Customer Agreement Form | Mandatory for every job. Must be completed and acknowledged before work begins. Filed and held for a minimum of 3 months.
Daily log review | Store Manager reviews the Alterations Log daily. Uncollected garments to be followed up within 48 hours.
Materials replenishment | Thread and materials inventory checked weekly. Replenishment requests submitted by Wednesday, delivered by Friday. Store Manager is accountable.
Weekly handover log | Outgoing tailor completes handover log (jobs completed, pending, awaiting collection, materials needed, open issues) at end of each rotation. Incoming tailor countersigns on first morning.
Garment packaging standard | All completed garments to be pressed and folded. Handed to customers in Vivo-branded bags. Applied consistently — no exceptions.
Revenue tracking | Paid and free-service revenue tracked separately in POS. Reported weekly. Free-service volume >30% of total triggers a threshold eligibility review.
POS setup | Alteration service code must be configured and tested at least 5 working days before go-live. IT/Systems must confirm receipt referencing prior to launch.
Damage & disputes | Any garment damage or dispute reported to Store Manager immediately. Liability capped at original purchase price. Escalation to Head of Retail for amounts above cap.
Secure storage | All customer garments to be stored securely in a designated, locked area when not being worked on.

12.  POLICY COMPLIANCE

Failure to adhere to this policy may result in:

Customer dissatisfaction and reputational damage to the Vivo brand
Financial loss due to damaged garments or disputed services
Operational disruption to the store and production unit
Disciplinary action where individual conduct is at fault

13.  GUIDING PRINCIPLE

The alteration service exists to support sales, improve garment fit, and enhance the customer experience.
It is not a full tailoring service and must be managed within defined operational limits at all times.

15.  TAILOR APPOINTMENT & ROTATIONAL TIMELINE

The Junction store will initially be served by a rotational secondment from the Sample Making team within Vivo's production unit. Four tailors will rotate on a weekly basis — one tailor per week — across the first four weeks of operation. Each seconded tailor will receive a transport allowance. At the end of Week 4, a permanent tailor will be appointed to be based full-time at the Junction store, drawing on the performance data from the rotation to inform the selection. Months 2 and 3 focus on embedding the permanent tailor and evaluating readiness for expansion.

PHASE | ACTIONS & MILESTONES
WEEKS 1–4 / Rotation & Setup | ▸  Identify and confirm 4 Sample Making tailors for the rotation pool — obtain HR & production approval / ▸  Define and communicate transport allowance rate, payment method, and claim process / ▸  Equip the Junction tailoring area: sewing machine, thread inventory (full Vivo colour palette), hand tools, pressing iron, tagged garment storage / ▸  Brief all 4 tailors on this policy, SOP, scope, customer interaction standards, and escalation routes / ▸  Publish the weekly rotation schedule (Tailor A: Wk 1 → Tailor B: Wk 2 → Tailor C: Wk 3 → Tailor D: Wk 4) / ▸  Activate the Alterations Log, garment tag system, Customer Agreement Forms, and POS service code before Day 1 / ▸  IT/Systems to confirm POS service code setup and receipt referencing at least 5 working days before launch
END OF WEEK 4 / Permanent Appointment | ▸  Compile the rotation performance summary: demand volume, alteration types, quality observations, handover issues, customer feedback / ▸  Select and confirm the permanent Junction tailor — preferred candidate from the rotation pool based on demonstrated performance / ▸  Communicate the appointment to the tailor, production unit, and Junction store management / ▸  Permanent tailor must be in place and operating from the start of Month 2 / ▸  Brief the permanent tailor formally: roles, responsibilities, escalation contacts, and performance expectations
MONTH 2 / Onboarding & Steady State | ▸  Permanent tailor operates full-time at Junction from Week 5 / ▸  Store Manager conducts weekly 1:1 check-ins for first 4 weeks post-appointment / ▸  Maintain daily logs; compile fortnightly summary report (jobs, SLA compliance, rework rate, customer feedback) / ▸  Finalise and activate the weekly materials replenishment cycle (Weds request → Fri delivery) / ▸  Assess production unit capacity impact and confirm secondment is not reducing Sample Making output / ▸  End of Month 2 check-in: Store Manager, permanent tailor, and production lead review operating model
MONTH 3 / Performance Review & Expansion | ▸  Compile the full 3-month performance report: jobs processed, revenue, free-service uptake, demand by day, rework rate, CSAT / ▸  Conduct permanent tailor performance review against quality, throughput, and CSAT benchmarks / ▸  Assess whether weekend/peak demand warrants ad-hoc supplementary cover from the Sample Making pool / ▸  Present performance report and expansion recommendation to Retail and Production leadership by end of Week 12 / ▸  Document lessons learned from the Junction pilot for use in any future store rollout

Transport Allowance — Terms

A daily transport allowance will be paid for every day a seconded tailor reports to the Junction store. The rate must be agreed and signed off by HR and Finance before the pilot commences.
Allowances are paid weekly via payroll or petty cash per company policy. Tailors must sign the daily attendance register at Junction for the allowance to be processed.
Absence must be reported to the Store Manager by 8:00 AM. The production unit is responsible for providing a replacement from the rotation pool where possible.
