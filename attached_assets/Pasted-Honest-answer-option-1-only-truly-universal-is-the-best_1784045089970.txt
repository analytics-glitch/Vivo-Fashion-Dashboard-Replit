Honest answer: option 1 (only truly-universal) is the best default, and here's why the other two are traps I'd only take if you have specific evidence they'd help.
Option 2 (bundle /api/me) sounds tidy but breaks the cache model. Universal data has one shape for everyone and can cache for hours. Identity data is per-user, changes on group edits, and expires on logout. Bundling them means either (a) the whole payload's TTL drops to whatever the identity part can tolerate, killing the universal-data win, or (b) you cache them separately internally and stitch — at which point you've just moved the parallel fetch inside the endpoint. The clean version is: /api/bootstrap (universal, long TTL, shared across users) called in parallel with /api/me (per-user, short TTL). Two requests instead of one, but each cached correctly. The perceived-latency win is nearly identical because they run in parallel — the slower of the two sets the wait.
Option 3 (include landing KPIs) is where "bootstrap" becomes a grab-bag. Landing KPIs vary by the user's default country/channel filters and by today's date — a much shorter TTL than "list of stores." You'd be dragging the whole endpoint's cache lifetime down to whatever the KPIs need, and you'd be recomputing them for every user on cold-cache hits even if they never look at the Overview page. If the Overview page has slow first-paint, the right fix is server-side rendering or an Overview-specific pre-warm, not stuffing it into bootstrap.
Option 1 keeps bootstrap doing one job well: universal lookups, cacheable for hours, small payload, low risk. If later you find the Overview's first paint is still slow, add a separate /api/overview/prewarm — different problem, different cache lifetime, different endpoint.
The heuristic: an endpoint should have one TTL story. Mixing universal + per-user + time-sensitive data into one response means the shortest-lived piece dictates the whole thing's cache behavior, and you lose most of the win.
So: go with option 1. If perceived load time is still bad after it ships, we'll have data to decide whether the follow-up should be an Overview-specific prewarm or something else entirely.
